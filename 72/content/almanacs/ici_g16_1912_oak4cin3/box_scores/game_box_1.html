Concepcion Error Sparks Four-Run Fifth; Reds' Rally Falls Short
Rain fell through the arc lights all evening, a thin persistent mist that slicked the infield grass and made the Coliseum feel like a place with no walls and no ceiling. For four innings neither Ken Holtzman nor Don Gullett, the Reds' young Kentucky left-hander who could still throw harder than anyone else in the building, allowed a runner past first base. Dave Concepcion broke it open in the fifth, lining a double into left field that was the hardest-hit ball of the night. Gullett bunted him to third, and Joe Morgan's fly to center brought him home.

Then Concepcion gave it back. Angel Mangual opened the bottom of the fifth with a hard grounder into the hole, and Concepcion couldn't handle it. Gene Tenace walked. Dick Green singled to score Mangual, and Holtzman bunted the runners to second and third. Bert Campaneris beat out a chopper to short on a 1-2 count to score Tenace, stole second without a throw, and came around behind Green when Joe Rudi lined a two-out single to right and the throw home arrived late. Only one of the four runs was earned. Gullett had allowed two hits in four innings and was losing by three.

Holtzman worked through the rain in a hurry. He struck out eight, scattered ten hits across nine innings, and threw 153 pitches because Dick Williams never so much as glanced at the bullpen phone. The ninth tested him. Denis Menke hit the first pitch of the inning 402 feet into the left-center bleachers, and Pete Rose tripled into the right-center gap. Hal McRae struck out. Concepcion, whose error had started all of it, doubled off the wall to bring Rose home, and took third when pinch hitter Cesar Geronimo flied out to center. Morgan walked on a full count, and the tying run stood at third with two outs. Bobby Tolan rolled the next pitch to Green at second, who took his time and threw across to first, and the East Bay crowd was already on its feet, hollering into the rain.
