# Universe 86 - Seven Lists

86 games. Same two starters every time. Boston brought a Hall of Fame third baseman and a 24-year-old who had just won the Cy Young and the MVP. Houston brought a thirty-nine-year-old Nolan Ryan and asked him to do something nobody has ever asked a starting pitcher to do.

He said yes.

The numbers say:

- Nolan Ryan struck out **717 batters** in 663⅓ IP. Roger Clemens struck out 541. They faced the same lineup.
- Roger Clemens - at the plate - went 9-for-225 with 156 strikeouts. He hit .040. Nolan Ryan, also at the plate, hit .071.
- Houston walked off Boston **11 times**. Bob Stanley was on the mound for **eight** of them.
- Bill Buckner hit eight home runs. **Seven** were off Nolan Ryan. The eighth was a two-out grand slam in the 9th inning, in a game where he had already taken Ryan deep in the 1st.
- Glenn Davis hit fourteen home runs. **Thirteen** were off Roger Clemens. Denny Walling hit fifteen. **All fifteen** were off Roger Clemens.

Seven lists.

---

## LIST 1 - One ball short of immaculate: a study in surrender

Strikeouts as the answer, every time. Boston's lineup was not a lineup of bums. Wade Boggs hit .315. Bill Buckner struck out 20 times in 363 at-bats. But against Nolan Ryan the bottom half of the order turned into three specific outs.

In 86 games nobody threw an immaculate inning. The closest anyone came was Ryan in the top of the 3rd of Game 77: Rice, Evans, Gedman, three strikeouts, **ten pitches**. The one ball went to Evans on the first pitch he saw. That was the only thing standing between Nolan Ryan and nine pitches.

1. **Game 66 - Ryan strikes out 15 in a complete-game shutout.** Boston gets 3 hits. Game time: 2:50. **Jim Rice goes 0-for-4 with 4 K.** He had four chances. Ryan took all four.
2. **Game 62 - Ryan strikes out 14 in a complete-game shutout.** 5 hits allowed. **Dave Henderson goes 0-for-4 with 4 K.**
3. **Game 56 - Ryan throws 10 innings on 104 pitches, strikes out 13, and loses 1-0.** Clemens throws 10 shutout innings back. With two outs in the top of the 10th, Don Baylor pinch-hit and put a 1-0 pitch 356 feet into the left-field seats. It was the only run of the night, and it was earned. Henderson struck out four times in this one too.
4. **Game 47 - Ryan strikes out 13 and leaves after 8 with a 6-3 lead.** Boston wins 7-6 anyway. Buckner had hit a 3-run homer off him in the 1st. In the 9th, with two outs and the bases loaded, Buckner hit a grand slam off Charlie Kerfeld. Ryan watched it from the dugout.
5. **Game 72 - Ryan 13 K in 7⅓.** Houston loses 2-1.
6. **Game 3 - 12 K.** Boston wins 6-3.
7. **Game 77 - 12 K, including the ten-pitch 3rd.** Boston wins 3-2.
8. **Game 51 - 11 K, 5 walks, 1 hit allowed.** Boston's only run scored in the 2nd on a Ryan wild pitch, with Roger Clemens at the plate and zero hits on the board. Ryan carried a no-hitter and a 1-0 deficit into the 8th, gave up a one-out single to Marty Barrett, and came out. Phil Garner pinch-hit a two-run homer in the bottom half. Houston won 2-1. Ryan got a no-decision.
9. **Game 82 - 11 K, 0 earned runs in 7 IP.** Houston wins 2-1.

Ryan averaged **8.34 strikeouts per start** and 9.73 per nine innings. In real 1986 he struck out 9.8 per nine. The simulator did not invent a better Nolan Ryan. It took the real one's rate and applied it to 663 innings instead of 178.

**Dave Henderson struck out 153 times in 295 at-bats, 51.9% of the time.** At least 123 of those came against Ryan. His first four-strikeout game was Game 21. There were four more: 23, 56, 62, 81. **Dwight Evans struck out 145 times in the same 295 at-bats**, and nobody talks about Dwight Evans.

The deep cut: **three times Ryan took a no-hitter deep into the night, and three times he got a no-decision.**

- **Game 10:** no-hit through seven with a 1-0 lead. Evans led off the 8th with a solo homer on an 0-1 pitch that tied it. Ryan went 8⅓, allowed that one hit, and Boston won in the 13th.
- **Game 51:** see above.
- **Game 54:** no-hit through six, but eight walks. Baylor pinch-hit a single in the 7th. Henderson scored on a throwing error when he stole third, and Baylor scored on a passed ball. Two unearned runs came off one hit, and Boston won 4-2.

Ryan had five career no-hitters by October 1986. He did not get a sixth here.

## LIST 2 - Glenn Davis kept hitting Roger Clemens

A list of duels, built around the **batter-pitcher pair**. 110 home runs were hit in this series. **60 were given up by Roger Clemens.** The names that did it kept doing it. Same pitcher. Same swing. Same result.

1. **Denny Walling vs. Roger Clemens: 15 of 15.** Every home run Denny Walling hit in 86 games came off Roger Clemens. He hit .307. Five of them were two-out solo shots in the 1st inning (Games 47, 69, 73, 76, 82), from the three-hole, before Clemens had broken a sweat. He hit two in Game 47.
2. **Glenn Davis vs. Roger Clemens: 13 of 14.** Games 11, 20, 23, 40, 46, 55, 58, 61, 64, 74, 79, 83, 84. The first led off the bottom of the 9th in Game 11 and tied it 1-1. Three batters later Houston won it on a Dwight Evans throwing error. Davis homered off exactly one other pitcher all series: Calvin Schiraldi, leading off the bottom of the 11th in Game 5, a walk-off.
3. **Kevin Bass vs. Roger Clemens: 10 of 10.** In 320 at-bats, Kevin Bass's home-run total against pitchers not named Roger Clemens was zero.
4. **Craig Reynolds vs. Roger Clemens: 7 of 8.** The eighth was the two-out, full-count walk-off off Bob Stanley in Game 53.
5. **Bill Buckner vs. Nolan Ryan: 7 of 8.** Games 6, 16, 34, 43, 47, 63, 76. A 3-run homer in the 8th of Game 34. A 3-run homer in the 1st of Game 47, followed eight innings later by the grand slam off Kerfeld. Buckner struck out 20 times all series. He was the one Boston hitter Ryan could not put away, and he kept making Ryan pay for trying.
6. **Dwight Evans vs. Nolan Ryan: 7 of 8.** The eighth came off Kerfeld in Game 71, five innings after Evans had homered off Ryan.
7. **Rich Gedman vs. Nolan Ryan: 6 of 7.** Two in Game 73, a solo shot in the 2nd and a 3-run shot in the 3rd. The seventh won Game 67 in the 11th off Larry Andersen.
8. **Wade Boggs vs. Nolan Ryan: 4 of 4.** Boggs hit .357 in real 1986 with eight home runs. Here he hit four. All against the same man.
9. **Jim Rice vs. Nolan Ryan: 4 of 4. Marty Barrett vs. Nolan Ryan: 3 of 3. Dave Henderson vs. Nolan Ryan: 4 of 5.** The same Dave Henderson who struck out 153 times homered off Ryan four separate times.
10. **Roger Clemens vs. Nolan Ryan: 1 of 1.** Game 2, leading off the 5th, 1-1 count, 398 feet. It was the second time he ever batted against this man. He took 223 more at-bats and never did it again. **Clemens batted 225 times. He struck out 156 times. He got 9 hits, and one of them was a home run off Nolan Ryan.**

This is a list and not a coincidence because of what happened with everyone else. The two starters gave up **98 of the 110 home runs.** Boston hit 43 all series and 38 came off Ryan. Houston hit 67 and 60 came off Clemens. The bullpens on both sides combined allowed twelve. Boston could only homer off Ryan. Houston could only homer off Clemens.

## LIST 3 - The Boston shutout, ranked by how rude it was

A list ranked by a **custom statistic**: Boston's humiliation, defined as **Nolan Ryan's strikeouts divided by game length in hours.** It measures how viciously and how *quickly* Boston was put away.

In 86 games, Boston was shut out **ten times.** Houston was shut out four. The Red Sox, with Wade Boggs and Jim Rice and Dwight Evans, scored zero runs ten different times.

10. **Game 37 - HOU 5-0.** Ryan 8⅓ IP, 6 K, 3:11. Rate: **1.89.** Clemens was gone after six. Polite, as shutouts go.
9. **Game 44 - HOU 2-0.** Ryan 6⅓ IP, 7 K, 3:21. Rate: 2.09. See the deep cut.
8. **Game 45 - HOU 1-0.** Ryan complete game, 7 K, 3 hits, 103 pitches, 2:58. Rate: 2.36. Houston's one run came in the 6th and Ryan never gave it back.
7. **Game 68 - HOU 4-0.** Ryan 8⅓ IP, 8 K, 3:14. Rate: 2.47.
6. **Game 8 - HOU 3-0.** Ryan complete game, 9 K, 5 walks, 3:06. Rate: 2.90. Clemens's first loss of the series.
5. **Game 83 - HOU 7-0.** Ryan complete game, 10 K, **143 pitches**, 3:18. Rate: 3.03. The most pitches he threw all series. He worked for this one.
4. **Game 79 - HOU 5-0.** Ryan complete game, 8 K, **94 pitches, 2:38.** Rate: 3.04. The fastest game in the series. It edges Game 83 by less than a hundredth, and it took forty minutes less to do it.
3. **Game 57 - HOU 3-0.** Ryan 8⅓ IP, 10 K, 2:52. Rate: 3.49. The same night, Clemens threw an eight-inning complete game on 96 pitches and lost it. See List 4.
2. **Game 62 - HOU 3-0.** Ryan complete game, **14 K**, 5 hits, 3:05. Rate: **4.54.**
1. **Game 66 - HOU 5-0.** Ryan complete game, **15 K**, 3 hits, **2:50.** Rate: **5.29.** This is the game that breaks the scale. It was the single best pitching performance in the dataset, thrown in 170 minutes.

The deep cut: **Game 44** finishes ninth on rudeness and first on absurdity. Ryan went 6⅓, did not finish, and **walked seven Red Sox.** Ryan handed out seven free passes, Boston collected 3 hits, and nobody scored. *Could you actually shut out the 1986 Red Sox by walking everybody?* Apparently you can.

Across the series Boston left **618 men on base** to Houston's 494. The Red Sox reached second base. They looked at it. They walked back to the dugout.

## LIST 4 - The Maddux: a complete game in under 100 pitches

A Maddux is a complete game thrown in fewer than 100 pitches. Greg Maddux, who the stat is named after, threw 13 of them as shutouts in a 23-year career. It requires that innings end on three or four pitches, that nobody walks, and that hitters swing early and get themselves out. Power pitchers who live on strikeouts do not throw Madduxes. Strikeouts take pitches.

In 172 starts between two strikeout pitchers, exactly **two** Madduxes were thrown. One of them was a win.

### 1. Game 79 - Nolan Ryan. **94 pitches, 9 innings, 0 runs.**

9.0 IP. 6 H. 1 BB. 8 K. A 5-0 Houston win in **2 hours, 38 minutes**, the fastest game of the series by nine minutes.

**Nolan Ryan.** The man whose reputation is the fastball nobody can hit and the walks nobody can explain. *That* Nolan Ryan threw 94 pitches in a nine-inning shutout and walked one guy. He averaged 10.4 pitches per inning. The Red Sox got six hits and could not move one of them across the plate.

### 2. Game 57 - Roger Clemens. **96 pitches, 8 innings, a complete-game loss.**

8.0 IP. 5 H. 3 R. 3 BB. 4 K. Kevin Bass homered in the 2nd. Billy Hatcher hit a two-run homer in the 3rd. That was all of it: Clemens gave up three runs on two swings and then held Houston scoreless for five more innings on almost no pitches. It still went down as a complete game, because Houston led after the top of the 9th and never had to bat again. Ryan shut Boston out on the other side.

This is the only way Roger Clemens could throw a Maddux in this series. When Boston was winning, Houston batted in the 9th and the bullpen got the ball. He went 8 innings on 92 pitches in Game 77, 92 in Game 78, and 89 in Game 40, and won all three, and none of them were complete games. The one time he finished a game in under 100 pitches, he had to lose it to do it.

Every Clemens entry on this table is a loss.

## LIST 5 - Some kinda vacation, huh pal?

Picture the guy. Forty-something. Boston accent. Real Red Sox fan, the kind whose father raised him on Yastrzemski stories. He flew his family down to Houston for Game 2 of the World Series. Stretched the budget. Got the kids out of school. Four seats in the upper deck of the Astrodome on a Sunday night in October.

In the 5th inning, Roger Clemens hit a home run off Nolan Ryan. Clemens pitched eight innings of one-run ball. Boston took a 3-1 lead into the bottom of the 9th. Bob Stanley came in. Houston scored three. The Astros won 4-3.

Then he saw it again.

Then he saw it again. Then again. Then again.

Then he tried to leave.

He could not leave.

Houston walked off Boston **eleven times** in 86 games. For the Astros fan with season tickets, it was the greatest stretch of home baseball ever played. For the Boston fan it was the same Sunday, with the same bullpen door opening, and the same man jogging out of it.

### The eleven walk-offs

1. **Game 2 (B9) - down 3-1, scored 3, won 4-3.** Off Stanley. Denny Walling doubled in the winning run.
2. **Game 5 (B11)** - Glenn Davis leads off the 11th with a homer off Calvin Schiraldi on an 0-1 pitch.
3. **Game 11 (B9)** - Davis leads off the 9th with a homer off Clemens to tie it 1-1. Stanley comes in. Bass singles, Cruz singles, Evans throws it away. Game over.
4. **Game 16 (B12)** - Alan Ashby hits a two-out, two-run homer off Stanley in the 9th to tie it. Houston wins in the 12th on a Hatcher sacrifice fly off Steve Crawford. The Boston tourist sat through twelve innings, paid Astrodome prices for a Coke and a hot dog, and watched his team lose 4-3 a few minutes after midnight, Boston time.
5. **Game 17 (B11)** - Kevin Bass strikes out, reaches on a passed ball, and scores on a Craig Reynolds single off Crawford. HOU 5, BOS 4.
6. **Game 18 (B9)** - Jose Cruz, two-run homer off Stanley, 0-2 count. HOU 5, BOS 3.
7. **Game 19 (B9)** - tied 3-3. Three singles off Stanley, two of them infield hits. The fourth walk-off in four games.
8. **Game 22 (B9) - down 2-0, scored 3, won 3-2.** Boston had been shut out for eight innings and scored twice in the top of the 9th. Houston answered with five hits off Stanley. The lead lasted ten minutes.
9. **Game 29 (B9) - down 4-2, scored 3, won 5-4.** Off Stanley. Two outs, Jim Rice drops a fly ball, two runs score, Stanley throws a wild pitch, Walling singles.
10. **Game 53 (B9) - Reynolds, two-out, full-count, solo walk-off homer off Stanley.** Tied 2-2, nobody on. The last possible swing.
11. **Game 73 (B9) - down 7-6, scored 2, won 8-7.** Off Stanley. Walling doubles in two. The tying run was Charlie Kerfeld, a relief pitcher, who had singled.

### The man on the mound

**Bob Stanley took the loss in eight of the eleven.** He also saved 22 games in this series. He was Boston's closer, and it went both ways every time. In real life, six nights after this Game 2, Bob Stanley threw the wild pitch in the 10th inning of Game 6 at Shea that let the tying run score. In this universe he threw one in Game 29, too.

### The three identical disasters

Three of those wins share a shape. **Down exactly two entering the bottom of the ninth. Scored exactly three. Won by exactly one.** Game 2. Game 22. Game 29. All three were off Bob Stanley.

### The two good nights

The Boston tourist did get two. **Game 4** and **Game 10** both went thirteen innings, and Boston won both by scoring in the 13th. Both nights, Houston's manager ended up handing the ball to an infielder. In Game 4, third baseman **Phil Garner** came in to pitch the 13th with the bases loaded, and all three runners scored. In Game 10, second baseman **Davey Lopes** came in to pitch and gave up a run-scoring single to the first man he faced. Game 10 was the longest game of the series at 4:52. Boston's two happiest nights in Houston came against infielders.

### The HOU 8th-inning supernova

The Astros woke up *late.* Across 86 games:

| Inning | BOS runs | HOU runs |
|--------|----------|----------|
| 6 | 14 | **36** |
| 7 | 31 | 27 |
| 8 | 26 | **41** |

The 8th was Houston's best inning, two runs ahead of the 1st. **Houston outscored Boston 77-40 in the 6th and 8th combined.** Most of that damage was not the bullpen. Clemens was usually still out there in the 8th; he threw at least eight innings in 66 of his 86 starts. At least 26 of those 41 eighth-inning runs came in games where he was on the mound for the whole inning. By the 8th he was usually past a hundred pitches, and Houston had seen all of them.

For the Astros fan with season tickets, this is paradise. You arrive at 7:00, you have a beer, you watch six quiet innings, and at some point in the eighth your team starts a rally and finishes it.

For the Boston tourist, it's the same thing in reverse, and he can't get back to Logan. The taxi he called only goes to the Astrodome. The flight he booked only flies to Houston. He files a complaint at the front desk of the Hyatt. The night manager smiles politely and explains that the lobby only has one exit, and it leads to the ballpark.

He puts the credit card down for another night and asks for a wake-up call. The wake-up call comes at the same time it came yesterday. It is Sunday. He goes to the same game tonight. The Astros score 3 in the 8th. Final score: HOU 4, BOS 3.

Some kinda vacation, huh pal?

## LIST 6 - The eight-game stretch where Nolan Ryan ate the World Series

From Game 15 through Game 22, Houston won eight in a row, the longest winning streak either team put together in the series. **Nolan Ryan was named Player of the Game in seven of them. He got the win in two.**

- **Game 15** - Ryan (8 IP, 1 R, W)
- **Game 16** - Ryan (9 IP, 10 K; 12-inning walk-off)
- **Game 17** - Ryan (7 IP, 1 ER; 11-inning walk-off)
- **Game 18** - Ryan (8⅓ IP, 8 K; walk-off)
- **Game 19** - Ryan (7 IP, 8 K; walk-off)
- **Game 20** - Glenn Davis (3-for-4, HR, go-ahead single in the 7th)
- **Game 21** - Ryan (8 IP, 1 R, W)
- **Game 22** - Ryan (8⅓ IP, 0 ER, 10 K; walk-off)

Five walk-offs in eight games, **four of them in a row** from Game 16 to Game 19. Across the eight starts Ryan threw 61⅔ innings with a 2.19 ERA and 62 strikeouts, and in six of them he left without a decision while his lineup finished the job. Game 20 was his only off night: 6 IP, 6 walks, 4 runs, and Houston came back to win it anyway.

The bookends: Game 15, Ryan goes 8 IP, 4 H, 1 R, 6 K, wins 3-1. Game 22, Ryan goes 8⅓ IP, 5 H, 2 R (0 ER), 10 K, and leaves in the 9th of a 0-0 game after an error and an intentional walk. Don Baylor pinch-hits a double off Kerfeld and both runners score. Houston gets three in the bottom half off Bob Stanley. Ryan is Player of the Game in a game he did not win.

Across the series, Ryan was named Player of the Game **46 times**, 53.5% of all games, in a series featuring a Cy Young/MVP on the other side and a future Hall of Fame third baseman. In the eight-game streak the rate was 87.5%.

There is no comparable streak in real baseball history, because no real pitcher has ever started 86 games against the same lineup on the same night. There is only the data, which says: **for eight straight Sundays in the middle of an endless World Series, Nolan Ryan owned the Astrodome.**

## LIST 7 - The Rocket, the labrum, and the man who showed up to throw 86 games

### Where Roger Clemens actually was, on October 19, 1986

To understand what the 86-game version of Roger Clemens means, you have to know the man the simulator picked up off his real-life mound and dropped into a loop.

He was **24 years old.** His third major-league season. His **first fully healthy one.**

His rookie year, 1984, ended early. In 1985 his shoulder gave out: **a torn labrum.** *Dr. James Andrews* cleaned it out arthroscopically in August 1985 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clemens). Pitchers in that era did not reliably come back from labrum trouble. Clemens came back.

On April 29, 1986, at Fenway against Seattle, he became **the first pitcher in major-league history to strike out 20 batters in a nine-inning game** (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clemens). He started the All-Star Game, won it, and was its MVP. He went **24-4 with a 2.48 ERA, 238 K, 254 IP** (baseball-reference.com/players/c/clemero02.shtml). He won the **AL Cy Young** and the **AL MVP**. No starting pitcher had won the MVP since Vida Blue in 1971; the next would be Justin Verlander in 2011 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clemens).

Then Boston went to the ALCS. The Angels jumped to a 3-1 series lead. Clemens started **three games in nine days**: Game 1 (Oct 7), Game 4 (Oct 11), and Game 7 (Oct 15), the last two on three days' rest (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_American_League_Championship_Series). He lost Game 1. He took a 3-0 lead into the 9th of Game 4, did not finish it, and watched the bullpen lose it in eleven. He won Game 7. **22⅔ innings, 4.37 ERA, 1-1.**

Game 1 of the World Series was October 18 at Shea. Clemens started Game 2 the next night on **three days' rest, again.** He was a 24-year-old, fourteen months out of shoulder surgery, who had thrown 254 regular-season innings and 22⅔ more in the ALCS.

He started Game 2 against Dwight Gooden. Boston led 6-2 in the bottom of the 5th. **He was pulled after 4⅓ innings, one out short of qualifying for the win, and got a no-decision** (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_World_Series). Steve Crawford got the W. The famous blister was Game 6, where he was lifted after seven and he and John McNamara later disagreed in public over whose idea it had been (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clemens). In the real record he did not finish either of his two World Series starts.

The simulator gave him **the ball every night for 86 nights.**

### The workload: 66 starts of eight innings or more

Roger Clemens pitched **at least 8 innings in 66 of his 86 starts.** Seventy-seven percent. He threw 10,228 pitches.

The longest run was **Game 18 through Game 30: thirteen straight starts of 8+ innings**, 1,601 pitches, about 123 per start. He went 3-5 with five no-decisions. **The losses were not on him.** He allowed 3 earned runs or fewer in twelve of the thirteen. The Boston offense scored 2 runs or fewer in five of them.

### The early run: no losses until Game 8

Clemens's first seven starts: **no losses.** Three wins, four no-decisions, 55 innings, 35 strikeouts, 2.45 ERA. His one blown lead was Game 2, when he left after eight with a 3-1 lead and Bob Stanley gave it away. He was **3-0** before he took an L. The series didn't humble him until Game 8, which he lost 3-0 to a Ryan complete-game shutout.

### The four-start no-HR streak

Clemens gave up **60 home runs** in the series. He gave up zero in **Games 13, 14, 15, and 16**, four straight starts and 33 innings. He went 2-1 with a no-decision. **It was the longest stretch of clean ball he had all series.**

### The complete games, both kinds

By the official scorer, Roger Clemens threw **33 complete games.** Twenty-six of them were eight-inning losses, because when Houston led after the top of the 9th, nobody pitched the bottom half.

The other seven went nine innings or more, and those are the ones that matter. He was **7-0 with a 0.70 ERA** in them, with four shutouts. **Game 71** was a two-hitter, the fewest hits he allowed all series. **Game 56** was the 10-inning 1-0 win over Ryan's own 10-inning complete game, the only 10-inning complete-game win in the dataset. **Game 38** was a 6-0 four-hitter. **Game 13** was a 1-0 six-hitter in which Boston also got only three hits. **When Roger Clemens was allowed to finish a baseball game, he won it.**

### The hot streak: g54 → g56, three Players of the Game in a row

His longest Player of the Game streak, and Boston's longest winning streak of the series: **three straight.** Game 54 (8 IP, 1 ER, won 4-2). Game 55 (8 IP, 3 ER, won 5-3). **Game 56 (10 IP, 0 R, won 1-0).** Three starts, 26 innings, 4 earned runs, **1.38 ERA**, capped by the best game he threw.

### The streak that broke him: six straight losses

From **Game 79 through Game 84**, six consecutive Clemens losses, the longest losing streak by either pitcher. He gave up 5+ earned runs in three of them. Game 81 was a 12-hit, 6-run nightmare. Game 83 he lasted 6⅓ and gave up 11 hits. By then he had thrown about 9,300 pitches in a series that wouldn't quit. **He still made the start every time.** He won Game 85.

### The number behind the W-L: 28 Players of the Game

Clemens finished **29-39 with 18 no-decisions.** He was named Player of the Game **28 times**, almost as often as he won. Houston ran on Ryan; Boston ran on Clemens. When the offense showed up, Clemens won. When it didn't, he kept pitching anyway. **3.07 ERA. 541 strikeouts. 675⅓ innings. 66 starts of 8+ IP.** A career year by any normal measurement, played out 86 times against the same nine guys, three of whom (Walling, Davis, Bass) hit 38 home runs off him and one off everybody else combined.

### The real-life coda

The flesh-and-blood Roger Clemens of October 1986 was a 24-year-old, fourteen months out of shoulder surgery, whose Game 2 World Series start lasted **4⅓ innings** before he was lifted with a four-run lead. The Roger Clemens the simulator built out of his data threw **675⅓ innings** of 3.07 ERA baseball across 86 starts, including thirteen straight 8-inning starts and a 10-inning 1-0 complete game. The shoulder the real Clemens had repaired in 1985 was, in this universe, asked to go to the mound on three days' rest eighty-six times, against the same lineup, behind a closer who kept giving up walk-offs.

It said yes every time.

The simulator does not know that Roger Clemens almost didn't have a career. It does not know about Dr. James Andrews. It knows only the shape of the data - the 24-4, the 2.48, the 238 - and it gave him 86 chances to be that pitcher. He took every single one. **The Rocket, in a universe that asked him to keep being The Rocket forever, did.**
