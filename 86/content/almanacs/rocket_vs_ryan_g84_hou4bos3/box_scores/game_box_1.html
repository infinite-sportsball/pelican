Astros Win World Series
With two out in the top of the eighth, the game tied 3-3, and runners on first and second, John McNamara let Roger Clemens hit for himself. Clemens had already left four men on base. He grounded to third for a force at second, and walked back out to the mound for the bottom half. Glenn Davis fouled off the first pitch and hit the second one 349 feet into the right-field seats. 4-3 Houston.

The runners Clemens stranded had gotten there the hard way. Dwight Evans singled off Larry Andersen to open the inning, and after Rich Gedman flied out, Dave Henderson fouled off seven pitches in a row with two strikes before lining a single into left, thirteen pitches in all. Hal Lanier brought in Charlie Kerfeld, who struck out Spike Owen, and that brought up Clemens.

Before all that it had gone back and forth. Houston scored twice in the first on four singles and a Clemens pickoff throw that went into right. Boston got one back in the second on a Spike Owen single, tied it on Bill Buckner's double in the third, and took the lead in the fifth when Wade Boggs doubled for the second time and Marty Barrett doubled him home. Kevin Bass led off the sixth with a triple and Alan Ashby beat out a ground ball to tie it. Nolan Ryan left in the seventh with 124 pitches and a no-decision. Clemens went all eight, 129 pitches, ten hits, and a loss, and went 0-for-4 at the plate with six men left on base.

In the ninth Boggs hit a fly ball to the deepest part of left-center, and it came off his bat like it had a chance, and then the dome air took the life out of it and Billy Hatcher caught it. Barrett and Buckner grounded out, and Houston had won 4-3. Kerfeld walked off the mound with the win, and McNamara stayed on the top step of the Boston dugout a while, looking at the spot in right field where Davis's ball had landed.
