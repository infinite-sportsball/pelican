Boston 1986 Takes Title
For seven innings nobody scored, and under the Astrodome lights, which throw no shadows and make the eighth inning look exactly like the first, it started to feel like nobody ever would. Roger Clemens and Nolan Ryan traded zeroes. Houston's one real chance came in the fifth, runners on the corners with one out, and it ended with Ryan striking out on a foul bunt and Bill Doran striking out behind him. Then Clemens struck out the side in the seventh and the first two men in the eighth. Five in a row.

Clemens also got a hit. In the third he lined a 1-2 pitch from Ryan through the left side for a single, his seventh hit in sixty-four nights of this. Seven. Sixty-four Sundays of a 24-year-old American League pitcher with no business holding a bat standing in against a man who throws 96, and seven times the ball has gone somewhere nobody could catch it. Wade Boggs singled him to second, and Marty Barrett grounded into his second double play of the night.

Boston broke through in the eighth, and it wasn't pretty. Boggs, who reached base five times, lined a double into deep left-center. Hal Lanier pulled Ryan after 127 pitches, and Bill Buckner greeted Larry Andersen with a single that sent Boggs to third. Then a pitch to Jim Rice got past Alan Ashby, and Boggs walked home. 1-0 on a passed ball. Ryan went 7⅓ innings, gave up one run, and took the loss.

In the ninth it came apart on Charlie Kerfeld. Dave Henderson led off with a home run into the right-field seats, and the bull on the scoreboard stayed in the barn. Then a walk, then Clemens topped a slow roller that Glenn Davis couldn't handle, then an intentional walk, then singles from Buckner and Dwight Evans around a Rice sacrifice fly. Clemens, of all people, scored one of the runs. It was 5-0.

Davis got one back with a homer to left in the bottom of the ninth, and the bull fired its pistols for forty seconds over a 5-1 game, which was about as much as it could do. Jose Cruz flied to Henderson in center for the last out. Clemens came off the mound with his glove already off: nine innings, eleven strikeouts, no walks, a single, and a run scored. His teammates met him at the top of the dugout steps, and the handful of Boston people behind the visitors' dugout made enough noise to be heard in a building that wasn't built for them.
