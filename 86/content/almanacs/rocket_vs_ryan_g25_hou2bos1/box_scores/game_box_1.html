Astros KO Red Sox
Inside the Astrodome, with Judge Roy Hofheinz's empty apartment and private putting green still tucked into the mezzanine, Houston won with two hard singles and eight innings of stubbornness, beating Boston 2-1 in a pitchers' duel that produced 13 hits and only three runs. Houston collected 11 of those hits, left nine men aboard, and scored both its runs with two outs, which is one way to turn plenty of traffic into barely enough supper.

The first run came in the first, after Houston's Billy Hatcher singled, teammate Denny Walling replaced him on a force play, and Astros first baseman Glenn Davis doubled Walling to third. Houston's Kevin Bass then lined Clemens's first pitch for a 105.6 mph RBI single. Boston's Roger Clemens, 24 years old and fifteen months removed from shoulder surgery, kept Houston there through seven despite all the baserunners, then finished eight innings with 11 hits, two runs, seven strikeouts, one walk, and 117 pitches. Houston's Nolan Ryan was 39 and no more interested in compromise: eight innings, two hits, one run, seven strikeouts, five walks, 120 pitches. He also struck Clemens out three times, because National League baseball occasionally makes an MVP pitcher carry a bat as punishment for being good at his actual job.

Boston finally got even in the eighth. Boston's Wade Boggs walked, teammate Marty Barrett bunted him over, and Red Sox first baseman Bill Buckner drove Ryan's next pitch toward right at 111.9 mph for a tying double. Houston answered before the inning was out. Davis doubled with one away, Bass drew an intentional walk, and Clemens got Jose Cruz to force Bass at second. On a 1-2 count, Astros catcher Alan Ashby chopped a 109.4 mph single through the right side, scoring Davis for the 2-1 lead. Eleven Houston hits had boiled down to that one ground ball.

Charlie Kerfeld replaced Ryan for the ninth and required 16 pitches to strike out Rich Gedman, Dave Henderson, and Ed Romero in order. Romero fought to a full count and fouled off two more before swinging through the last pitch, which finished in Ashby's mitt. Houston had won a World Series game, not a championship, and the noise from 46,101 people hit the sealed roof and came back down on Ashby with the baseball still in his hand.
