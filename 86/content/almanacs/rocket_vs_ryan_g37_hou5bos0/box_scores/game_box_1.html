Astros Beat Red Sox in World Series
Inside the Astrodome, where the temperature never changed and a cheer had to bounce off the roof before it could go anywhere, Houston took the lead before Nolan Ryan had worked up a proper sweat. Astros second baseman Bill Doran singled off Boston's Roger Clemens, stole second, and scored when Denny Walling punched a 102.5 mph grounder through the right side. Boston had its chances against Ryan, because even a power pitcher occasionally leaves men standing around: Dave Henderson doubled with two out in the second, and Wade Boggs and Bill Buckner singled in the third. Ryan stranded all four of them, including Jim Rice on three pitches, and the evening settled into a wire-to-wire Houston shutout.

Clemens was 24, with a labrum that had been repaired fourteen months earlier, and for six innings Houston made him look like a young man stuck with an old man's assignment. Billy Hatcher led off the third by driving a 1-0 pitch 354 feet to left. In the fifth, Hatcher stole second and scored on Walling's 110 mph double, then Astros right fielder Kevin Bass hit a 1-1 pitch 336 feet to right for two more. Twice the animated bull fired its pistols, twice the cowboys and spaceships went through their forty-second routine, and Clemens finished with nine hits, five runs and two homers allowed on 92 pitches. He also struck out trying to bunt against Ryan, which was about as much fun as his night required.

On this Sunday in the loop, Ryan gave Boston 128 pitches and eight and a third innings, allowing four hits and six strikeouts while the Red Sox carried their sixty-eight-year wait from one scoreless inning to the next. A ninth-inning error put Rice aboard, but Ryan struck out Dwight Evans before Houston reliever Larry Andersen recorded the final two outs around a Henderson walk. When Andersen covered first on Spike Owen's last grounder, the noise struck the roof and came back onto a Houston club holding a 5-0 Game 2 win, with Bass's fifth-inning swing still sitting plainly in the middle of it.
