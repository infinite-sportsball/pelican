Houston 1986 Astros Win World Series
Under the Astrodome's flat lights, Nolan Ryan stood in Houston's rainbow-gut uniform and made a one-run lead feel like a week's wages. The Houston right-hander threw a three-hit shutout on 103 pitches, struck out seven, and never let Boston put two hits in the same inning. Roger Clemens was nearly as hard on Houston: seven innings, five hits, nine strikeouts, and one run, though five walks helped run Clemens's count to 131. This was a pitchers' duel with no decorative scoring, and Houston won it 1-0.

The whole game turned in the sixth. Houston first baseman Glenn Davis drew a leadoff walk from Clemens, Kevin Bass struck out, and Jose Cruz took a 2-2 pitch into left field at 112.5 mph. Davis reached third, kept going, and scored without a throw. One walk, one double, one run. That was the entire offense that mattered. Clemens also went to the plate twice and struck out twice, which is what happens when a league makes its best pitcher carry lumber against Nolan Ryan.

Boston had chances small enough to fit in a scorebook margin. Red Sox second baseman Marty Barrett doubled to open the seventh and stayed there while Bill Buckner flew out, Jim Rice struck out, and Dwight Evans popped up. Rich Gedman singled to begin the eighth and went nowhere. Calvin Schiraldi kept Houston at one in the bottom half despite a Davis double and an intentional walk, giving the Red Sox one last honest turn.

Ryan took that turn himself. Wade Boggs walked to start the ninth, Barrett bunted him to second, and Buckner walked, putting the tying run in scoring position with one out. Rice flew to right. Evans then swung through a 0-2 pitch for Ryan's seventh strikeout, the ball finishing in Alan Ashby's mitt while the dome's noise struck the roof and came back down. Houston's young franchise had won a World Series game, 46,101 people had a sound worth keeping, and Buckner walked back to the visitors' clubhouse.
