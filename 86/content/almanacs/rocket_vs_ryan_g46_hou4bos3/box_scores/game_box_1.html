Houston 1986 Defeats Boston 1986
Nolan Ryan went seven innings on 131 pitches, which for a 39-year-old man is the kind of number a trainer writes down twice, and he spent a fair share of them missing the plate. Six walks. Ten strikeouts. Boston put a runner on in seven of nine innings and stranded ten, and by the end the Red Sox looked like a team that had been pickpocketed in a well-lit room. Houston won 4-3, a one-run seesaw that changed hands twice.

Houston's Jose Cruz started it in the second, lining a 3-2 pitch into right for a double that scored Kevin Bass, then came home on Alan Ashby's sac fly. Boston answered in the third: Wade Boggs walked, Marty Barrett pushed a grounder through the right side, Bill Buckner lifted a sac fly, and Jim Rice turned on a 1-0 pitch and hit it 360 feet to left, which in the still, dead air of the Astrodome is about as far as a baseball wants to go. Then Ryan singled to right off Clemens, Bill Doran forced him at second, stole second himself and scored on a Clemens wild pitch. Three apiece.

Clemens went seven, four runs on five hits, two wild pitches, and exactly one strikeout, a pinch-hitting Davey Lopes, which is a strange line for a man who struck out 238 that summer. At the plate he went 0-for-3 with three strikeouts, one of them a two-strike bunt fouled off. Glenn Davis led off the bottom of the sixth, ran the count full, and put the 3-2 pitch 406 feet into the left-center seats. That was the fifth home run Davis had hit off Roger Clemens in this loop. Five separate Sunday nights, every one a solo shot, same first baseman, same pitcher, nobody on base to share it with.

Larry Andersen walked Boston's Dave Henderson to open the eighth, let him reach third, and struck out Boggs looking on a full count. Calvin Schiraldi set Houston down in order. Then Charlie Kerfeld needed seven pitches in the ninth, and Rice, whose one swing had been Boston's whole evening, went down swinging on a 1-2 pitch for his fourth strikeout of the night. He stood a second looking at the ball in Ashby's mitt while 46,101 people rose at once, and every one of Judge Hofheinz's spring-loaded seats snapped shut behind them like a round of applause.
