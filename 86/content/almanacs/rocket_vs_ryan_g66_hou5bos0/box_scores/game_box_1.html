Houston 1986 Beats Boston 1986 in World Series
Nolan Ryan beat out an infield single in the third inning, and when Bill Doran lined the next pitch into the right-center gap, Ryan, 39 years old, ran from first all the way home. Doran took third on the throw and scored on Billy Hatcher's sacrifice fly. Houston had a 2-0 lead, and one of the runs belonged to a man who was about to have a night nobody else in this loop would ever have.

Ryan struck out fifteen Red Sox. Fifteen is the most anybody struck out in eighty-six of these Sundays, and he did it four nights after he'd struck out fourteen. Every Boston hitter who came to the plate struck out at least once: all nine in the lineup, and both pinch hitters too. Wade Boggs, who almost never strikes out. Roger Clemens. Don Baylor, sent up with two on in the seventh, on three pitches. Jim Rice struck out four times. Boston had three hits all night, two of them by Bill Buckner. Upstairs, somewhere in the mezzanine, Judge Roy Hofheinz's apartment sat empty with its barber's chair and its putting green, four years after the Judge died, and he would have liked this one. A Texan throwing 96 in a Texas building he'd built to keep the weather out.

Clemens kept it close for six innings, and the seventh got away from him. Jose Cruz doubled, Alan Ashby was walked on purpose, and Craig Reynolds doubled down the first-base line. Then another intentional walk to load them, and Clemens hit Hatcher with a pitch to force in a run, and Denny Walling lifted a sacrifice fly. 5-0. Clemens left after seven innings and 95 pitches, 0-for-2 at the plate, and Mike Greenwell struck out batting for him.

Buckner led off the ninth with a double to right-center, and Ryan struck out the side behind him. Rice swung through strike three. Dwight Evans swung through strike three. Rich Gedman took a 1-2 pitch on the outside corner, and the umpire rang him up, and the ball was in Ashby's mitt before Gedman had finished looking at it. Fifteen. The Astrodome crowd was on its feet, and Ryan walked toward Ashby, wiping his face on the shoulder of his rainbow stripes, a 39-year-old who had just done the best thing anybody was going to do on this night, in this building, in eighty-six tries.
