Boston 1986 Red Sox Are Champs
Nolan Ryan walked Jim Rice and Dwight Evans on eight straight pitches in the second inning, and for most of the night he was two different pitchers taking turns. One of them struck out thirteen Red Sox. The other one walked six, and that one decided the game. In the fourth Ryan walked Bill Buckner, gave up a double to Rice, walked Evans, and then walked Rich Gedman to force in a run. Then he struck out Dave Henderson and Spike Owen and got Roger Clemens on a ground ball, and walked off the mound having given up one run in an inning where he could have given up four. In the fifth Marty Barrett doubled and Buckner doubled him home on the first pitch. 2-0.

Clemens pitched like a man trying to keep the ball on the ground and mostly managing it. Houston hit fifteen ground-ball outs against him and three fly-ball outs. The Astros loaded the bases with two out in the fifth on two singles and a hit batsman, and Clemens struck out Billy Hatcher. They put runners on the corners in the eighth, and he struck out Jose Cruz looking. He went eight innings on 103 pitches and struck out six, and at the plate he went 0-for-3 with two strikeouts, part of Ryan's thirteen. The only run came with two out in the sixth, when Kevin Bass hit a 2-1 pitch 411 feet into the right-center seats. Kevin Bass hit ten home runs in this loop, every one of them off Roger Clemens, and this was the tenth. There wouldn't be an eleventh. Somewhere in eighty-six Sundays of this, the thing that kept happening just stopped, and nobody noticed, least of all Bass, who trotted around the bases in his rainbow stripes while the bull fired its pistols one last time on his behalf.

Bob Stanley took the ninth. Alan Ashby lined out to short and Craig Reynolds flied to right, and then, down a run, with two out and a bench full of hitters, Hal Lanier let Charlie Kerfeld bat. Kerfeld stood in, a big man in rainbow stripes holding a bat, against a Boston closer in road grays, and grounded Stanley's first pitch to short. Owen threw to Buckner, and it was over, 2-1, and forty-six thousand people in the Astrodome sat and looked at their manager.
