Boston 1986 Triumphs in World Series
For six innings the Astrodome offered the kind of argument pitchers enjoy and everybody else has to sit through, Roger Clemens working with a shoulder repaired fifteen months earlier and Nolan Ryan still throwing hard at 39 because apparently nobody had told his right arm what age it was. Boston scratched out a third-inning run when Red Sox third baseman Wade Boggs doubled and second baseman Marty Barrett singled him home. Houston answered in the fifth after Astros outfielders Kevin Bass and Jose Cruz singled, then Bass came home on a Clemens wild pitch. It was 1-1, and neither starter looked interested in asking for help.

Boston changed the terms in the seventh. Red Sox left fielder Jim Rice reached down in an 0-2 count and lined a double to left, Ryan intentionally walked Dwight Evans, and catcher Rich Gedman got a 2-1 pitch he could handle. He sent it 374 feet into the left-field seats at 92.5 mph, enough work to beat the Astrodome's heavy indoor air. Because Gedman wore Boston gray, the giant bull kept both pistols holstered while the line score quietly became 4-1. In this loop, even five hits over a complete game could purchase Ryan a loss. He finished all nine on 115 pitches with eight strikeouts and one walk.

Clemens carried that lead into the ninth with 121 pitches behind him, only two strikeouts, and an 0-for-3 night at the plate that included two strikeouts of his own. Bass opened with a 106.9 mph triple, Cruz walked, and Boston called for Bob Stanley. Ashby's sacrifice fly scored Bass, then Astros shortstop Craig Reynolds drove a triple to deep right that brought Cruz home and placed the tying run ninety feet away. Stanley caught Phil Garner looking before Bill Doran hit a 110 mph grounder directly to shortstop Spike Owen for the last out.

Boston won 4-3, but Game 2 is not a championship and the Red Sox had still been waiting since 1918 for one of those. This moved the possibility one game closer. The Boston bench made its noise, the small pocket of traveling fans answered beneath the sealed roof, and Stanley walked off with a save while Reynolds remained at third, the run Houston needed still standing beside him.
