Roger Clemens came up in the fifth with Spike Owen on first and one out, and tried to bunt him over, and missed, and fouled one off, and missed again. Strike three, on a bunt. Wade Boggs came up next and hit a 1-1 pitch from Nolan Ryan 402 feet into the left-center seats, and a man on first with one out had turned into two runs anyway. 2-1 Boston, and it stayed that way.

Houston's run came in the second. Glenn Davis doubled down the third-base line on the first pitch he saw, took third on a Clemens wild pitch, and scored on a Kevin Bass single. After that Clemens gave Houston almost nothing. He went eight innings, gave up five hits, and needed 92 pitches to do it. Twice in this loop he got through eight innings on exactly 92 pitches, one run allowed, a win, and the two nights ran back to back. Same arm, same count.

Ryan was nearly as good and lost anyway. He went eight, struck out eleven, struck out the side in the sixth, and walked five. The strangest moment of the night was his. In the eighth Bill Buckner led off with a double into the right-center gap, and with Dwight Evans aboard on an intentional walk, Buckner, 34 years old, ankles taped so thick he ran like a man wading a creek, took off for third. On AstroTurf. Alan Ashby threw him out by plenty.

Houston put two on in the eighth and didn't score, and Bob Stanley came in for the ninth. He struck out Bass, got Jose Cruz on a ground ball, and then Ashby fouled off four pitches with two strikes before lifting a fly ball to right. Evans caught it, and Boston had won 2-1. Clemens was on the top step of the dugout, and Buckner, the man who'd tried to steal third, was the first one out to shake Stanley's hand.
