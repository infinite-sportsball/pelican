Astros Top Red Sox
Boston's Roger Clemens had three runs to work with before he threw a pitch, which ought to have been plenty. Wade Boggs singled off Nolan Ryan, Bill Buckner lined a double down the first-base line to score him, and with two out Dwight Evans turned on a 1-2 pitch and hit it 398 feet to left-center. The three-story scoreboard in center, built to throw a party for Astros home runs, did nothing. A 3 went up next to Boston on the line score, and that was the whole ceremony.

After that Ryan, working like he had a plane to catch, quit giving up runs and started giving up everything else. Houston made three errors behind him. Dave Henderson walked four times, never logged an official at-bat, and got stranded at third on three of those trips. Boston left the bases loaded in the fourth and fifth, and Clemens helped, striking out with men on second and third in the fourth, one of three he took on the night. Ryan went eight on 131 pitches, seven strikeouts, five walks, and never let it get worse than 3-0.

Through seven Clemens had allowed three hits, and Rich Gedman had erased two Astros baserunners himself. Then Alan Ashby singled on a full count, Craig Reynolds singled, and Phil Garner, hitting for Ryan, walked to load the bases with nobody out in the eighth. Bill Doran's fly to left scored one. Billy Hatcher took strike three. Then Denny Walling, starting at third, jumped an 0-1 pitch and put it 383 feet to left, 4-3 Houston. It was Walling's eighth home run of this series. All eight had come off Roger Clemens, across seven different Sunday nights. The bull fired its pistols for the first time all night, and Clemens took a new ball and got Glenn Davis on a fly to center.

Charlie Kerfeld needed twelve pitches for the ninth. Jim Rice fouled off two full-count pitches, then lifted one to Hatcher in center, and a franchise that had never played in a World Series had won a game in one, its winning pitcher watching from the dugout. Clemens sat at the far end of the visitors' bench with a towel over his shoulder, a complete game and 127 pitches behind him, all four runs in one inning, looking down at the carpet.
